How Your Oral Final Is Graded

A one-on-one conversation in Spanish, at your sign-up time. The dates are on the course calendar and the weight is in the syllabus.

You draw two topics: one about you, your day, your family or friends, and one about free time, plans, a trip or today. I ask 8 questions: 2 warm-up questions (¿Cómo te llamas? ¿De dónde eres?) and 3 from each topic. While you talk, I count five things over those 8 questions, and each count gives points.

The five things I count

  • Understanding the questions (20 points). How many of the 8 questions you understand the first time, without a repeat.
  • Answering in Spanish, in sentences (25 points). How many answers are a full Spanish sentence with a conjugated verb: ¿Qué estudias? → Estudio biología.
  • Accuracy of the forms asked for (20 points). How many answers have a wrong form of what the question asks for: ¿Duermes? → duermo, not duermes. At most one per answer.
  • Saying more (20 points). How many answers add a detail or a reason: Estudio biología porque es interesante.
  • Pronunciation: being understood (15 points). How many words I can't understand because of how they're said. An accent is fine.

How the counts work

  • An answer with no Spanish (in English, no answer, or No sé) counts as an error in Accuracy and as a word I couldn't understand. Saying nothing can't earn those points.
  • A memorized answer that doesn't answer the question doesn't count as understood or as an answer.
  • Follow-up questions count. If I ask ¿Por qué? or ¿Y tu familia? and you answer with a full sentence that adds something, that counts as saying more (once per question).
  • Fixing your own mistake isn't an error, and neither is a correct answer with a different verb.
  • No answer in Spanish to any question, or not present: 0.

Not counted against you: Asking ¿Puede repetir? or ¿Cómo se dice…?: it's a strategy (a repeat counts only in Understanding). An accent, as long as the word is understood. Mistakes in grammar beyond Chapters 1–5.

How to raise each score

  • Understanding the questions: Have a lab assistant ask the practice questions in random order until you understand each one the first time.
  • Answering in Spanish, in sentences: Answer every question with a full sentence that has a verb: ¿Qué haces? → Hago la tarea.
  • Accuracy of the forms asked for: Check the verb form for each question: change the person (¿Tienes…? → Tengo…), stem changes (duermo), irregular yo (salgo), ser vs. estar.
  • Saying more: After each answer, add one thing with y, pero, porque or también.
  • Pronunciation: being understood: Practice your most-used words aloud with a lab assistant, slowly, with Spanish vowels (a, e, i, o, u).
